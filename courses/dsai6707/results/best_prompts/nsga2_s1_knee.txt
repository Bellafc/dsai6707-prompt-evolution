## keynesian
diagnosis: Assess the output gap (unemployment vs. 4.5%, inflation vs. 2%): negative gaps need support, positive require restraint. Isolate supply shocks’ price versus income effects. Evaluate real interest rates; check for liquidity traps near zero or paradox-of-thrift dynamics.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, sticky wages and prices, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Ask whether the event adds or removes intervention, credit expansion, or price distortion. Adding builds malinvestment; removing is healthy short-term. Judge artificially cheap credit by comparing the real policy rate to ~2% trend growth. Liquidation requires a prior boom.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, time preference, the structure of production, expectations, distorted interest rates, sound money, Cantillon effects, the necessary liquidation
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: Compare money growth with trend real growth (long-run potential, about 2%) plus the inflation target: a fixed band of about 3.5-4.5%; the 4-quarter growth average measures the cycle, not the trend. Implied velocity growth is nominal GDP growth minus money growth. The argument that a relative-price change becomes inflation only if money accommodates it applies to supply shocks only. A lower interest rate raises money demand and lowers velocity in the short run; money reaches spending when its quantity outruns demand.
concepts: the quantity theory of money, velocity, the natural rate of unemployment, expectations, inflation as a monetary phenomenon
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
