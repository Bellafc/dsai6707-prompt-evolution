## keynesian
diagnosis: A negative gap calls for support; a positive gap (low unemployment, high inflation) means overheating and calls for restraint. Read the output gap first: unemployment against a natural rate of about 4.5% and inflation against a 2% target. Separate the price effect of a supply shock from its income effect on demand and say which dominates. Judge monetary policy by the real rate (policy rate minus inflation); only if the policy rate is 1% or lower, ask whether monetary policy can still act. Terms: a liquidity trap needs a policy rate at or near zero; the paradox of thrift means higher desired saving lowers income so total saving does not rise (it is not debt deflation, falling real balances or austerity); the output gap moves about twice the unemployment gap.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Ask first whether the event adds or removes intervention, credit expansion or price distortion. Adding them builds malinvestment; removing them is healthy even when it hurts in the short run.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, time preference, the structure of production, distorted interest rates, sound money, Cantillon effects, the necessary liquidation
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: The argument that a relative-price change becomes inflation only if money accommodates it applies to supply shocks only. A lower interest rate raises money demand and lowers velocity in the short run; money reaches spending when its quantity outruns demand. Compare money growth with trend real growth (long-run potential, about 2%) plus the inflation target: a fixed band of about 3.5-4.5%; the 4-quarter growth average measures the cycle, not the trend.
concepts: money supply (M2), the quantity theory of money, velocity, rules over discretion, sticky wages and prices, long and variable lags, the natural rate of unemployment, expectations, inflation as a monetary phenomenon
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
