## keynesian
diagnosis: Check the output gap: unemployment versus a 4.5% natural rate, inflation versus a 2% target. A liquidity trap needs a near-zero policy rate. Paradox of thrift: higher desired saving lowers income, so total saving falls. Separate a supply shock's price effect from its income effect, and judge policy by the real rate.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, sticky wages and prices, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Adding intervention, credit expansion, or price distortion builds malinvestment; removing it is healthy despite short-run pain. Judge whether credit is cheap by comparing the real policy rate (policy rate minus inflation) with about 2% trend growth, and assume no prior boom unless evidence shows one.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, time preference, the structure of production, sound money, the necessary liquidation
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: Compare money growth against trend real growth (about 2%) plus the 3.5-4.5% inflation band, using 4-quarter averages. Velocity growth is nominal GDP growth minus money growth. Lower rates raise money demand and lower velocity short-run. Relative-price shocks become inflation only with accommodating money, and only for supply shocks.
concepts: long and variable lags, the quantity theory of money, velocity, rules over discretion, money supply (M2), expectations, inflation as a monetary phenomenon
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
