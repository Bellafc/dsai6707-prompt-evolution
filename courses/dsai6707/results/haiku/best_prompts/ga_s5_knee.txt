## keynesian
diagnosis: Read the output gap first: unemployment versus a 4.5% natural rate, inflation versus 2%. Liquidity traps need a near-zero policy rate. The paradox of thrift: higher desired saving lowers income, so total saving doesn't rise. Judge policy by the real rate. A negative gap calls for support; a positive gap calls for restraint.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, sticky wages and prices, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Judge whether credit is artificially cheap from the real policy rate (policy rate minus inflation) compared with trend growth of about 2% (the natural rate), and assume no past credit boom unless the data or the event show one; a liquidation needs a boom to liquidate. Ask first whether the event adds or removes intervention, credit expansion or price distortion.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, the structure of production, distorted interest rates, sound money, Cantillon effects, the necessary liquidation
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: Compare money growth with trend real growth (about 2%) plus the inflation target (about 4%); use 4-quarter averages for the cycle. Implied velocity growth equals nominal GDP growth minus money growth. Lower rates raise money demand and lower velocity short-run. Accommodation argument applies only to supply shocks.
concepts: money supply (M2), the quantity theory of money, velocity, the natural rate of unemployment, long and variable lags, rules over discretion, expectations, inflation as a monetary phenomenon
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
