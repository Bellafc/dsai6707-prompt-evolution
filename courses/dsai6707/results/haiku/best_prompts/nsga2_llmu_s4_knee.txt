## keynesian
diagnosis: Begin by assessing the output gap, comparing unemployment with a natural rate near 4.5% and inflation with a 2% target. Gauge the stance of monetary policy through the real rate, meaning the policy rate minus inflation, and consider whether policy still has room to act only when that policy rate sits at 1% or below. When the gap is negative, the economy needs support; when it is positive, with unemployment running low and inflation running high, the economy is overheating and restraint is the appropriate response.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, sticky wages and prices, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy, velocity
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Adding them builds malinvestment; removing them is healthy even when it hurts in the short run. Ask first whether the event adds or removes intervention, credit expansion or price distortion.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, the quantity theory of money, time preference, the structure of production, distorted interest rates, sound money, Cantillon effects, the necessary liquidation
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: Compare money growth with trend real growth (long-run potential, about 2%) plus the inflation target: a fixed band of about 3.5-4.5%; the 4-quarter growth average measures the cycle, not the trend. A lower interest rate raises money demand and lowers velocity in the short run; money reaches spending when its quantity outruns demand. Implied velocity growth is nominal GDP growth minus money growth. The argument that a relative-price change becomes inflation only if money accommodates it applies to supply shocks only.
concepts: rules over discretion, the quantity theory of money, velocity, money supply (M2), long and variable lags, the natural rate of unemployment, expectations, inflation as a monetary phenomenon
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
