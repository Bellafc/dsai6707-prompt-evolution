## keynesian
diagnosis: Read the output gap via unemployment against a 4.5% natural rate and inflation against a 2% target. Negative gap calls for support; positive calls for restraint. Separate a supply shock's price effect from its income effect and say which dominates. Judge policy by the real rate; check for a liquidity trap only at a policy rate near zero.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, sticky wages and prices, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Ask whether the event adds or removes intervention, credit expansion, or price distortion; adding them builds malinvestment, while removing them is healthy despite short-run pain. Compare the real policy rate (policy rate minus inflation) with the roughly 2% natural rate, and assume no prior boom unless evidence shows one.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, time preference, the structure of production, distorted interest rates, sound money, Cantillon effects, the necessary liquidation, liquidity trap
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: Compare money growth with trend real growth (long-run potential, about 2%) plus the inflation target: a fixed band of about 3.5-4.5%; the 4-quarter growth average measures the cycle, not the trend. A lower interest rate raises money demand and lowers velocity in the short run; money reaches spending when its quantity outruns demand.
concepts: money supply (M2), the quantity theory of money, inflation as a monetary phenomenon, rules over discretion, long and variable lags, the natural rate of unemployment, expectations, velocity
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
