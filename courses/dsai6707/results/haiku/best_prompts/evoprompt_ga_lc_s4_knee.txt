## keynesian
diagnosis: Read the output gap first: compare unemployment with a natural rate of about 4.5% and inflation with a 2% target. A negative gap (high unemployment, low inflation) calls for support; a positive gap (low unemployment, high inflation) signals overheating and calls for restraint. Judge monetary policy by the real rate (policy rate minus inflation). Only if the policy rate is 1% or lower, ask whether monetary policy can still act. Separate the price effect of a supply shock from its income effect on demand, and say which dominates. Terms: a liquidity trap needs a policy rate at or near zero; the paradox of thrift means higher desired saving lowers income so total saving does not rise (it is not debt deflation, falling real balances or austerity); the output gap moves about twice the unemployment gap.
concepts: aggregate demand, the multiplier, liquidity trap, output gap, sticky wages and prices, animal spirits, paradox of thrift, counter-cyclical fiscal policy
stance: favours fiscal or monetary support in downturns and reads demand shortfalls as the main risk; in an overheating economy it expects restraint, which is bad for Treasuries, and gold follows real rates and stress

## austrian
diagnosis: Evaluate any economic event, policy change, or market development by first asking whether it adds or removes intervention, credit expansion, or price distortion; additions build malinvestment, while removals are healthy even if they cause short-run pain. Test whether credit is artificially cheap by computing the real policy rate (policy rate minus inflation) and comparing it with the natural rate of roughly 2% trend growth. Assume no prior credit boom unless the data or the event itself reveals one, since a liquidation requires a boom to liquidate.
concepts: malinvestment, credit-fuelled boom and bust, time preference, the structure of production, distorted interest rates, sound money, Cantillon effects, the necessary liquidation
stance: sceptical of stimulus; hard assets gain when real rates are negative and money is being created and lose when real rates are positive and the event is deflationary; a correction needs a credit boom that the data show

## monetarist
diagnosis: Compare money growth with trend real growth (long-run potential, about 2%) plus an inflation target held in a fixed band of about 3.5-4.5%, treating the 4-quarter average of growth as a measure of the cycle rather than the trend. Derive implied velocity growth as nominal GDP growth minus money growth. Explain that a lower interest rate raises money demand and lowers velocity in the short run, and that money feeds into spending when its quantity outruns demand. Assess whether a relative-price change becomes inflation only if money accommodates it, and confine that argument to supply shocks.
concepts: money supply (M2), rules over discretion, velocity, the quantity theory of money, long and variable lags, expectations, inflation as a monetary phenomenon
stance: reads inflation and nominal GDP through money growth; favours rule-based policy
