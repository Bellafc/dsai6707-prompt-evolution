Classical PSO Algorithm
• PSO is a robust stochastic optimization technique based on the movement
  and intelligence of swarms
• PSO applies the concept of social interaction to problem solving
• It was developed in 1995 by James Kennedy (social-psychologist) and
  Russell Eberhart (electrical engineer)
• It uses a number of agents (particles) that constitute a swarm moving
  around in the search space looking for the best solution
• Each particle is treated as a point in a N-dimensional space which adjusts its
  “flying” according to its own flying experience as well as the flying
  experience of other particles
• Each particle keeps track of its coordinates in the solution space which are
  associated with the best solution (fitness) that has achieved so far by that
  particle. This value is called personal best, pbest

• Another best value that is tracked by the PSO is the best value
  obtained so far by any particle in the neighborhood of that particle.
  This value is called gbest
• The basic concept of PSO lies in accelerating each particle toward
  its pbest and the gbest locations, with a random weighted
  acceleration at each time step

                            Classical PSO
> The inertia weight w is often chosen between [0.5, 1.5].
> A large inertia weight (w) facilitates a global search while a small inertia weight
  facilitates a local search.
> By linearly decreasing the inertia weight from a relatively large value to a small
  value through the course of the PSO run gives better PSO performance as
  compared with fixed inertia weight settings.
         Larger w ----------- greater global search ability
         Smaller w ------------ greater local search ability.
> The interval of c1 and c2 is [0, 4.0]. If c2 is higher than c1, the social information
  would influence the particle more. If c1 is higher than c2, the particle would be
  affected more by its personal best rather than the global best.
> Rand() is a random number between [0, 1].

Comparison with other evolutionary computation techniques:

 • Unlike in genetic algorithms, evolutionary programming and
   evolutionary strategies, in PSO, there is no selection operation
 • All particles in PSO are kept as members of the population
   through the course of the run.
 • No need of crossover or mutation operations in PSO
 • In PSO, balance between the global and local search can be
   adjusted through the inertial weight factor (w).

                    Differential Evolution
• Proposed by Price and Storn in 1995.
• Considered as one of the most powerful evolutionary algorithms for real
  number function optimization.
• Differential Evolution -- a simple and efficient adaptive scheme for global
  optimization over continuous spaces, 1995.
• Finished 3rd at the First International Contest on Evolutionary
  Computation, Nagoya, 1996.
• Outperformed GA and PSO on a 34-function test suite (Vesterstrom &
  Thomsen, 2004).
• Continually exhibited remarkable performance in competitions on
  different kinds of optimization problems like dynamic, multi-objective,
  constrained and multi-modal problems held under IEEE Congress on
  Evolutionary Computation (CEC) conference series.

                Different DE Mutation Schemes




The general convention used for naming the various mutation strategies is
DE/x/y/z, where DE stands for Differential Evolution, x represents a string denoting
the vector to be perturbed, y is the number of difference vectors considered for
perturbation of x, and z stands for the type of crossover being used.

                        Memetic algorithms

• MA are evolutionary algorithms that incorporates global search
  (crossover + mutation) and local searches simultaneously.


• Metaphors in natural systems:
    • Evolutionary adaptation (GA) across generations, AND
    • Individual learning within a lifetime of an individual.


• Individual learning can be implemented as (problem-specific) local
  searchers within MA.

                     Preservation of Diversity

• The use of local search in MA can easily lead to a loss of
  diversity:
    – All points / individuals are moved to a local optimum

• Allow only a small fraction of the population undergo local search.

• Use multiple local searchers to induce different search space and distinct
   local optima

• Prevent duplicates in the selection operator

• New acceptance criteria for local search, e.g., Boltzmann method (similar
   to Simulated Annealing).
