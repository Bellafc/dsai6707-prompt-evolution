How to write a paper: Introduction
• Paradigm
• Sentence 1 and Sentence 2: Establish the
  importance of this research topic, and provide
  general background information.
• Sentence 3: Do the same as in Sentences 1 and 2,
  but in a more specific/detailed way.
• Sentence 4: Describe the general problem area
  of the field.
• Sentence 5 and Sentence 6 : Provide the
  literature review.
• Sentence 7: Describe a gap in the research.
• Sentence 8 and Sentence 9: Describe the paper
  itself, and give details about the methodology
  reported in the paper.
• Sentence 10: Announce the findings.
How to write a paper: Methodology

• Provide an overview of the method itself.

• Provide details about what was done and continues to describe what was done in detail.

• Mention a possible difficulty in the methodology.

How to write a paper: Review

           Treat every review like gold dust
    Be (truly) grateful for criticism as well as praise

   ü Read every criticism as a positive suggestion for
     something you could explain more clearly
   ü DO NOT respond “you stupid person, I meant X”. Fix
     the paper so that X is apparent even to the stupidest
     reader.
   ü Thank them warmly. They have given up their time for
     you.

How to present a paper
Don’ts


     Don’t put     everything in the poster.

    Don’t leave your poster during the poster session.

    Don’t stand in front of your poster.

    Don’t read your poster to your audience but talk to them.

    Don’t bore     your audience with a long walkthrough, no more than 3 minutes.
How to present a paper
Dos


          Use           Use your poster as a tool for communicating with your audience.


       Prepare          Prepare a brief but informative way to present the information in your poster.



        Stand           Stand to the left of your poster at the beginning of your walkthrough and to the
                        right of your poster at the end.



      Stand by Stay by your poster to meet and interact with poster session attendees.
How to present a paper

                        • Introduce yourself and your research topic.
                        • Provide a brief introduction to the study (perhaps 2-3 sentences)
                            e.g. “Previous studies have demonstrated A, but seldom efforts have been
                            made in the intersection of A and B. The goal of the research is to narrow
                            this research gap”.
                        • Explain briefly the methods you used.
                        • Describe results with a rationale conclusion
                           e.g. “We first decided to examine... To investigate this we...
                           We found that... This means that...”.
                        • Summarize the main conclusions of your study, usually 2-4
       Effective          points.
                        • Provide a brief statement about future directions.
