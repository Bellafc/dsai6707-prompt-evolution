  Hill Climbing
Hill climbing algorithm goes uphill along the steepest possible path until
can go no further up, which may return a state that is a local maximum.

Hill Climbing
            Pros:
            Avoid traversal and achieve the purpose of improving efficiency.
            Cons 1:
            Hill climbing will not necessarily find the global maximum, but
            may instead converge on a local maximum.

Hill Climbing
            Pros:
            Avoid traversal and achieve the purpose of improving efficiency.
            Cons 2:
            In plateau cases, the hill climber may not be able to determine in
            which direction it should step, and may wander in a direction that
            never leads to improvement.

The 4 queens problem consists of a 4 x 4 chessboard with 4 queens. The goal is to place
the 4 queens on the chessboard such that no two queens can attack each other. Each queen
attacks anything in the same row, in the same column, or in the same diagonal.

                 • State: 4 queens on the board. One queen per column. (�0 , �1 , �2 , �3 )
                 – Variables: �0 , �1 , �2 , �3 where �� is the row position of the queen in
                 column i. Assume that there is one queen per column.
                 – Domain for each variable: �� ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}, ∀i.
                 • Initial state: a random state.
                 • Goal state: 4 queens on the board. No pair of queens are attacking each
                 other.
                 • Generation of a solution: Swap the row positions of two queens.
 (2,0,3,1)       • Objective function: The number of pairs of queens attacking each other,
                 directly or indirectly.
